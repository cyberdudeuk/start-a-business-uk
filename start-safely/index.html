Start and run a business safely

Keeping your name, address and activity away from people who should not find you

Starting a business, training or performing can put your name and address on public records. If someone is trying to find or control you, plan what becomes public before you register anything. This guide is for survivors of domestic abuse, stalking or harassment, and for anyone whose safety depends on staying hard to find.

Who this is for

  • Survivors of domestic abuse, stalking or harassment, of any gender
  • People living in a refuge or other safe accommodation
  • Anyone helping a survivor, such as a support worker or business adviser

If you need help now

In danger now: call 999. All helplines below are free and open 24 hours unless stated.

If you cannot speak on a 999 call

From a mobile, dial 999, listen to the operator, and cough or tap the phone if you can. If you are prompted, press 55 so the operator knows it is a genuine emergency. Staying silent without pressing 55 can end the call.

Make your devices and accounts safe first

Someone may be able to see your phone, email or location. Changing settings can alert them, so plan with a support service.

Use a device and an email account the other person cannot reach

Do your business research and set-up on a device they have never had access to, with a new email account and a phone number that is only for the business.

Location and connected accounts

Phones, cloud accounts, social media and home devices can share your location. Refuge's tech safety guides take you through turning off location sharing, changing passwords and removing connected devices, step by step.

Plan before you change anything

Refuge warns that some things you do to increase your security or privacy might alert an abuser. Save any evidence first, and make a safety plan with a domestic abuse service before you change settings or delete anything.

Choose how to set up, knowing what becomes public

A sole trader is not on the Companies House register. A limited company is, with some details protected by default and more that you can apply to protect.

As a sole trader

You register with HMRC, which does not publish your details. Your invoices must show your name, and if you trade under a business name they must also give an address where legal documents can be delivered to you. That address does not have to be your home.

As a limited company: shown by default

The register shows each director's name and service address. Your home address and full date of birth are not published on the register. The company's registered office address is public.

The registered office

It must be an 'appropriate address' where delivery can be acknowledged. A PO Box is no longer allowed. You can use a third-party agent's address if it meets the rules, which keeps your home off the register.

Apply to protect more

If you are at serious risk, a director or person with significant control can apply to stop Companies House giving their home address to credit reference agencies. A person with significant control can apply to have all their information kept off the public register. It costs £100. You cannot protect your home address if it is the company's registered office.

Apply before you register

If you can, apply for protection before your details reach Companies House. Removing information already published is harder than never publishing it. The police and other specified public authorities can still ask Companies House for protected information.

Changes in the law

The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 set out wider protection, for example for a refuge's address. Check Companies House's current guidance for what you can apply for today.

Other public records to watch

Your address can reach the public through the data protection register, the electoral register, licences and online listings.

The data protection fee register

Most businesses that handle personal information pay the Information Commissioner's Office a data protection fee, and the ICO publishes the name and address on a public register. If you work from home, give a PO Box or other address instead, or call the ICO helpline on 0303 123 1113 to keep a home address off the register.

The electoral register

If your safety would be at risk, you can register to vote anonymously, so your name and address are not shown. Being anonymous can make credit checks harder: you get a certificate of anonymous registration that you can show credit reference agencies or a bank.

Licences and professional registers

Some licences and professional registers are public. Before you apply, ask the licensing body or regulator what they publish and whether an address can be withheld for safety.

Websites, listings and reviews

Map listings, domain registrations, social media and review sites can show where you work. Use a business address that is not your home, and check what each one makes public before you set it up.

Training, exams and performing

Courses, exam boards and venues hold your details too. Ask what they share before you enrol or perform.

Enrolling on a course or exam

Use your safe email and address. Ask the provider who sees your details, whether names appear on attendance lists, certificates, results lists, photos or social media, and how to opt out.

Performing or working in public

You can work under a professional or trading name, but contracts and legal documents still need your legal name. Ask venues, agents and organisers not to publish your schedule or location in advance.

If something is published by mistake

Ask the organisation to remove it straight away, and tell your support service so they can update your safety plan.

Words used on this page

The same definitions are used across the 360º portals (shared glossary, version 1.0.0).

Service address
The address a company director gives Companies House for post. It is public, and does not have to be the director's home.
Registered office
A company's official address. It is public and must be an 'appropriate address' where delivery can be acknowledged; a PO Box is not allowed.
Anonymous registration
Being on the electoral register without your name or address being shown, for people whose safety would be at risk. It can affect credit checks.
