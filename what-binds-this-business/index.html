    Describe the business, see what binds it

    This is the service this guide said was missing: activity in, regulation out, with the instrument, the regulator, the deadline and the guidance to act on. It is deliberately small and honest about its edges.

    The same thing, for machines

    The rulebook is published three ways: as records, as a read-only API description, and as an MCP server manifest so an assistant can query it directly with provenance attached. The Open Regulation Document Standard imagined regulation as enriched machine-readable data; this goes a step further by making it callable and by carrying verification with every record.

      Method, and what would make this real

      Each rule was written by hand from verified guidance, then linked to the instrument on legislation.gov.uk, whose URI was checked on 17 September 2026. Each carries its regulator, jurisdiction as ISO 3166-2 GB codes, activity as UK SIC sections and divisions, the guidance a business can act on, and a verification result. The licence finder and Business Companion are linked from the rules they belong to, because they are the authoritative routes for licences and trading standards law.

      To be a real service this would have to be generated, not written: rules derived from legislation and regulator data, maintained by the bodies that own them, and published under something like the Open Regulation Document Standard so that no one has to hand-build it again. That is the argument, and this is the smallest working demonstration of it.
