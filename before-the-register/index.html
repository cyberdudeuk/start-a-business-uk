    Three lines that don't line up

    Registration is one line, but founders deal with at least three. They don't always coincide, which helps explain why founders are unsure when obligations begin.

      What this suggests for data and metadata

      This guide's own proposals, for discussion.

      1. Measure the linePublish registrations started and completed, identity checks completed before incorporation, and time from first visit to registration, for both routes.
      2. Define "ready to register" as dataHold the requirements as a maintained, machine-readable checklist that GOV.UK, business.gov.uk, GOV.UK Chat and formation software all read, so no front door omits a step.
      3. Mark content by its side of the lineTag guidance as before, at or after registration, and by route, so search and AI answers stop mixing pre-start advice with post-registration obligations.
      4. Carry what founders give across the lineWith consent, reuse verified identity, the checked name and the activity description when HMRC registrations follow, building on the joint service assessed in 2018.
      5. Version the lineRecord each requirement with the date it took effect, so content, checklists and research can be dated against the line they describe.
      6. Register research against the lineTag each study with its zone, route and population, so the gaps above are visible before new research is commissioned.

        The boundary is the moment a business registers: incorporation with Companies House for a company, or registration for Self Assessment with HMRC for a sole trader. Studies were found through the GOV.UK Search and Content APIs, 25 GOV.UK blogs and parliamentary reports. Findings are paraphrased; short quotations are attributed. Zones, pain points and ratings are an editorial reading of the evidence.
