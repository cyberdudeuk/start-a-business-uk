      Identity and identifiers: the data spine the journey lacks

      A limited company that employs, imports and tenders accumulates a separate reference from almost every body it meets, and signs in through several systems to use them. The joint Companies House and HMRC accounts filing service, one of the few places a company filed once for two bodies, closed on 31 March 2026.

      Four nations, one registry

      Company registration and tax are UK-wide; almost everything around them varies by nation. The variation is felt unevenly: 64% of Northern Ireland businesses in DBT's survey called understanding differences between UK nations a burden, against 20% in England, though the Northern Ireland sample was small .

        Method, evidence grading and sources

        This guide walked the journey stage by stage from GOV.UK and business.gov.uk, following each handoff to the body that owns the next step. It was then re-tested against evidence, replacing adviser and trade sources with primary sources wherever one could be found.

        Touchpoint tags: verified means confirmed on a primary government, regulator or official-statistics source; reported means the key figure rests on professional, trade or press sources; baseline means a long-standing requirement not re-verified on these pages. Findings carry a separate strength rating.

        This search used two machine-readable sources. The GOV.UK Search API was queried for eight phrase variants ("start a business", "set up a business", "start your business", "new business owners", "business start-ups" and their stems), returning 1,497 unique pages. The WordPress API of 25 GOV.UK blogs returned 8,593 posts from 2009 to 2026, matched locally against start-up, Business Link, business.gov.uk, helpline and incorporation patterns. Matches were then read and verified by hand; the counts include incidental mentions. The UK Government Web Archive could not be queried automatically because it requires human verification, so Business Link-era pages are evidenced through GDS posts and published evaluations rather than the archive itself.

        Not covered in depth: charity and community interest company routes, individual sector regulators, planning, individual council portals and the territorial extent of the Commercial Payments Bill. Where official sources conflict, both are shown rather than reconciled.
