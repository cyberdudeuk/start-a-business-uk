Starting out, or starting again

For young people, women, and adults coming back after time away

Running your own business is one way into work, beside a job, learning or training. This guide shows what changes with your age and situation, the support made for you, and where to learn or retrain first if you want to. If your safety depends on staying hard to find, start with Start and run a business safely.

Who this is for

  • Young people aged 16 to 24, including those not in work, learning or training, and care leavers
  • Young women and women of any age
  • Adults coming back after raising a family, caring, a sabbatical or illness, prison, or the armed forces

If you are 16 to 24

You can be a company director from 16. Most start-up money starts at 18. Support made for young people runs to 25 in Wales and to 30 through The King's Trust.

Age 16 and 17

You can be appointed a company director from 16, but you cannot get a Start Up Loan until you are 18. At this age, learning or training alongside a small idea is usually the stronger start.

Start Up Loans, from 18

A government-backed personal loan of £500 to £25,000 for a business that has been trading for less than five years, with free mentoring. You need to be 18 or over and live in the UK. You can apply while getting Universal Credit.

The King's Trust Enterprise programme, 18 to 30

Free workshops, one-to-one support and the chance to apply for funding, across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

In Wales, 25 and under

Big Ideas Wales, part of the Young Person's Guarantee, offers free advice and a Young Person's Start Up Grant of up to £2,000. The grant particularly targets young people who are unemployed or not in education or training.

In Scotland, 18 to 30

The First Minister's Start Up Challenge, a 2025 Programme for Government commitment run with The King's Trust, offers an accelerated entrepreneurial apprenticeship for young people from disadvantaged or under-represented backgrounds. Check gov.scot for whether it is open.

On Universal Credit

If self-employment is your main job, you may get a start-up period of up to 12 months, when your actual earnings are used and the minimum income floor does not apply. You will need to show your work coach you are building the business.

Care leavers

You can ask your personal adviser to include starting a business in your pathway plan, alongside work, learning and training.

Young women and women of any age

Most help is open to everyone. Women-specific funding exists, but it is aimed at innovative or fast-growing businesses and is very competitive.

Women in Innovation Awards

Innovate UK runs awards for women founders. In the 2026 round, 61 winners each received a £75,000 grant and 12 months of business support, in advanced manufacturing, digital and technologies, and life sciences. Places are few, so treat it as one option, not a plan.

Investment for women-led businesses

The Invest in Women Taskforce, backed by the British Business Bank, is building funds that invest in women-led businesses. It suits businesses seeking equity investment, not most first-time founders.

Lenders and investors

Firms that sign the Investing in Women Code commit to improving access to finance for women. Ask a lender or investor whether they have signed.

Childcare, caring and confidence

Childcare, being a single parent and caring responsibilities are covered in Your circumstances. Women's networks and communities are listed in Skills and communities.

Safety

If someone could use your business details to find you, read Start and run a business safely before you register anything.

Learn or retrain first, at any age

Nothing requires a qualification to register a business, but skills change what you can do and charge. Free and funded courses for adults exist in every nation.

England: English and maths

If you are 19 or over and do not have GCSE English or maths at grade 4 (C) or equivalent, you are legally entitled to free study up to and including level 2. Free essential digital skills courses are also available.

England: a first level 3 qualification

Free Courses for Jobs: if you are 19 or over and do not have a level 3 qualification (equivalent to A levels), you can choose from over 400 free courses.

England: Skills Bootcamps

Free, flexible courses lasting up to 16 weeks, in areas such as digital and construction.

Wales: Personal Learning Accounts

Fully funded part-time courses for people aged 19 or over living in Wales, including employed and self-employed people and full-time carers, unpaid ones included. Some courses have an earnings limit.

Scotland

Skills Development Scotland's My World of Work has careers advice and Individual Training Accounts that help pay for a course. Check the current eligibility there.

Northern Ireland

The Careers Service on nidirect gives free, impartial careers advice and information about courses.

Free careers advice

National Careers Service (England), Careers Wales, My World of Work (Scotland) and the Careers Service (Northern Ireland).

Coming back after time away

Whatever kept you away, the same first steps apply: check how earnings affect any benefits, refresh skills if you want to, and use the support made for your situation.

After raising a family or caring

How self-employment works with Universal Credit and with caring is in Your circumstances. In Wales, Personal Learning Accounts are open to full-time carers, including unpaid carers.

After a sabbatical or illness

If a health condition or disability affects your work, Access to Work can cover self-employment; Your circumstances has the detail.

After prison

In England and Wales, prisons have Employment Hubs where staff and partners help with job and training plans before release. What a criminal record changes when you start a business is in Your circumstances.

After the armed forces

The Career Transition Partnership is the official resettlement service, usually from two years before discharge to two years after. Standard and Enhanced Learning Credits can help pay for training. Veterans' support is in Your circumstances.

Words used on this page

The same definitions are used across the 360º portals (shared glossary, version 1.0.0).

Start-up period
Up to 12 months on Universal Credit when your actual self-employed earnings are used and the minimum income floor does not apply.
Minimum income floor
An assumed level of earnings Universal Credit uses for gainfully self-employed people once any start-up period ends, if they earn less than it.
